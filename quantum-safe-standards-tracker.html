Quantum-Safe Standards Tracker

Quantum computers will eventually break the public-key cryptography that protects most digital systems today. This tracker follows the post-quantum and quantum-safe security standards that seven bodies are publishing in response.

NIST sets the cryptographic algorithms. IETF sets the protocol layer. IEEE covers link-layer security. ISO/IEC provides the international framework. ETSI provides the European framework and quantum key distribution device standards. ITU-T sets quantum key distribution network standards. IMDA sets Singapore's national reference specifications for quantum key distribution networks. Each entry is tagged by lifecycle stage, so the register can support procurement, migration planning and technical reference alike.

Regulatory anchors & migration timelines

Standards define what quantum-safe looks like. These government and sector mandates set when it has to be in place. They are the compliance clock behind procurement and tender language. They are shown here for context, not as standards-body deliverables.

Scope. This is a curated snapshot of the principal quantum-safe deliverables from NIST, IETF, ETSI, ISO/IEC, ITU-T, IEEE and IMDA. It is not an exhaustive registry. IETF entries include both published RFCs and active working-group drafts. Draft status changes frequently, so the linked datatracker page is always the authoritative source. ETSI deliverables are reachable by document number from the TC CYBER Quantum-Safe Cryptography working group and ISG QKD. ISO/IEC items sit under JTC 1/SC 27. ITU-T QKD network recommendations sit under Study Group 13. SSH and QUIC entries are filed under IETF. MACsec and MKA entries are filed under IEEE 802.1. All four sit under category "Protocol", so filtering to that category shows the transport and link-layer picture together. IMDA entries are Singapore's national reference specifications, listed under the IMDA Telecommunication and Security Standards page.

Before relying on any item for compliance, confirm the current version, stage and effective date at the source. Draft numbers, ISO stage codes and expected final dates change over time. Symmetric primitives, such as AES-256, are treated as quantum-safe with adequate key length. Other cryptographic functions, such as hash functions, are also out of scope here.
